# C9 C1 via determinant degree: derive or disprove

Task: task-432. Owner: qside. Date: 2026-10-25.
Natural-language derivation/obstruction; no extra assumptions; no Lean;
no mixed/E3; only this file written.

## 0. Definitions

Assume the three source inputs:

A1 = Theorem 3.11: the multiradial construction algorithm, possible output
     data O in 1,circ U, IPL/SHE/APT, and Ind1/2/3.
A2 = Proposition 3.9: packet/procession-normalized log-volume, local
     normalization, global degree = log-volume, procession average.
A3 = Remark 3.9.5: holomorphic hull as minimal lambda·O region, determinant
     construction E_H, det^tensor M, O(-) twist, and the degree/log-volume
     comparison.

The missing compatibility C1 is:

    C1. There exists a possible output D0 in O (modulo Ind1/2/3) such that
        logVol(D0) = Q, where Q = -|log q| is the q-pilot log-volume.

Equivalently: Q lies in the set R_possible of log-volumes of possible output
data.  C1 is exactly the xi-e/xi-f compatibility issue.

## 1. Verdict

C1 is NOT derivable from A1+A2+A3 by determinant degree.  The determinant
degree identity of A2/A3 is a statement about the hull H and its rank>1
arithmetic vector bundle E_H:

    deg(det E_H)/M = mu_log(H) = h_can = -|log Theta|,

not an existence statement about an element D0 of the possible-output set O
with degree Q.

The correct conditional statement is:

    A1 + A2 + A3 + C1  =>  Q <= h_can  (Cor3.12/P6_can).

Without C1, the determinant degree identity gives only the comparison of the
hull's degree with the q-pilot's degree (and only in the normalized sense); it
does not produce an output D0 of degree Q.

This is an exact gap, not a missing numerical calculation.

## 2. What the determinant degree actually gives

### 2.1 Hull-level identity

A3 supplies the hull H = phi(O) and a rank>1 arithmetic vector bundle E_H;
Ob3/det^tensor M gives a line bundle

    L_H = det^tensor M(E_H) tensor det^tensor M(O(-))^{-1},

and A2 gives, relative to the chosen normalization nu,

    deg_nu(L_H)/M = deg_nu(det E_H) = mu_log_nu(H) = h_can.      (2.1)

For the q-pilot line bundle L_q, A2 gives deg_nu(L_q)=Q.

Thus the determinant degree computes two real numbers, h_can and Q, and (2.1)
is an identity about the hull.  It does not assert that Q is attained by any
element of O.

### 2.2 Output-level question

C1 is an existence statement:

    exists D0 in O such that logVol(D0)=Q.

The determinant construction applies to a **hull/region**, not automatically
to an element of the possible output set.  There is no source statement:

    det(E_{D0}) is the q-pilot line bundle L_q for some D0 in O,

nor:

    the determinant-degree map from O onto R is surjective at Q,

nor:

    the q-pilot object itself is a possible output in O.

A1 says the q-pilot is the fixed input and O is the possible output data
linked to it by full poly-isomorphisms of F^value x mu-prime-strips.  A link
of prime-strips is not a numerical equality of log-volumes, and it does not
make the input q-pilot an output element.

## 3. Attempted derivations and why they fail

### Attempt 1: take D0 = H (the hull)

Then logVol(D0)=mu_log(H)=h_can, not Q.  A2/A3 give h_can, and Cor3.12 itself
would give only Q<=h_can.  Equality h_can=Q is not a consequence of
A1-A3; it is precisely the missing C1/xi-f step.  Taking D0=H also assumes
the hull is an element of O, which is not stated; the hull is formed from O,
not necessarily itself a possible output.

### Attempt 2: take D0 such that det(E_D0)=L_q

This would give C1 by the degree identity.  But no A1-A3 theorem identifies
the q-pilot line bundle L_q with the determinant of an output's E_D0.  The
determinant comparison in Ob3 compares the **hull** determinant to the
q-pilot; it does not produce a preimage object inside O.  This is exactly the
missing output-to-q-pilot identification.

### Attempt 3: use a scaling of the hull to force degree Q

One can formally scale a region by lambda to change its log-volume by
log(lambda), but:
- the scaled region is not necessarily a possible output in O;
- O is not stated to be closed under arbitrary scaling;
- choosing lambda = exp(Q-h_can) would be an arbitrary extra region, not a
  source possible image.
This would be an additional assumption, prohibited by the task.

### Attempt 4: use the ratio identity only

A2's normalized determinant identity is a **ratio**:

    deg_nu(L_H)/(M mu_log_nu(H)) = 1,

invariant under a common positive rescaling of log-volumes and degrees.  It
does not fix the absolute value Q or exhibit an output of that value.  A
ratio identity cannot imply an existence statement about a member of O.

## 4. Exact obstruction

The determinant degree construction is a **functional identity on the hull**:

    mu_log(H) = deg(det E_H)/M,  and  Q = deg(L_q).

C1 requires a **membership/surjectivity statement**:

    Q in { logVol(D) | D in O }.

A1-A3 provide:
- the output collection O and its link to the q-pilot input (A1);
- the normalized log-volume and degree=log-volume (A2);
- the hull/determinant identity (A3).

They do not provide:
(G1) a map from O to the q-pilot line bundle whose determinant has degree Q;
(G2) a proof that the determinant-degree map on O has Q in its image;
(G3) a proof that the hull H or a scaled hull is an element of O;
(G4) a numerical equality h_can=Q (which would make D0=H work);
(G5) an absolute normalization linking Q and h_can beyond the ratio identity.

These are exactly the missing ingredients.  In particular, A2/A3 cannot
supply (G1)-(G3): determinant degree computes the degree of the object it is
applied to; it does not create an object of a prescribed degree.

## 5. Conditional theorem (valid)

Theorem 5.1.  Assume A1, A2, A3, and C1.  Then

    Q <= h_can.

Proof.  C1 gives D0 in O with logVol(D0)=Q.  The hull H of the union of
possible output data contains D0; normalized log-volume is monotone, so
h_can=logVol(H)>=logVol(D0)=Q.  QED.

This is the correct use of C1.  It does not prove C1; it shows C1 is
sufficient for the xi-f inequality.

The converse does not hold: Q<=h_can does not imply the existence of D0 in O
with logVol(D0)=Q.  A lower bound on the hull volume does not exhibit an
element attaining it.

## 6. Why determinant degree cannot close C1

A determinant degree is an additive functional on line bundles.  To use it to
prove C1 one would need one of:

(D1) an identification L_q = det(E_{D0}) for an explicit D0 in O;
(D2) a surjectivity theorem for the determinant-degree map from O onto the
     relevant interval of R;
(D3) an equality h_can=Q plus a proof that the hull is an output element of O.

Neither A1 nor A2/A3 supplies (D1)-(D3).  The source's hull/determinant
comparison is a comparison at the level of normalized log-volume functionals;
it compares the **hull** to the q-pilot, not an output of O to the q-pilot.
The closed loop/xi-f argument is exactly an attempt to supply D1/D2; until it
is expanded, it is C1 itself, not a consequence of determinant degree.

## 7. Classification

PROVED/EXACT:
- A2/A3 give the normalized determinant-degree identity for the hull:
  deg(det E_H)/M = h_can.
- A2 gives deg(L_q)=Q for the q-pilot line bundle.
- C1 + A2/A3 + monotonicity => Q<=h_can.
- Determinant degree alone does not imply C1.

NOT DERIVED / OPEN:
- existence of D0 in O with logVol(D0)=Q (C1);
- identification of L_q with a determinant of an output;
- surjectivity of the determinant-degree map from O at Q;
- hull membership in O or h_can=Q.

WITHDRAWN:
- deriving C1 from the determinant degree identity;
- using a scaled hull as an output without proving it is in O;
- replacing C1 by the inequality Q<=h_can (circular).

## 8. Conclusion

C1 cannot be derived from A1+A2+A3 via determinant degree.  The determinant
degree identity computes h_can for the hull and Q for the q-pilot, but C1 is
an existence/membership statement about a possible output D0 in O.  The
missing step is exactly the output-to-q-pilot compatibility (xi-e/xi-f), not a
consequence of the degree formula.  The correct conditional statement remains:

    A1 + A2 + A3 + C1  =>  Q <= h_can.
