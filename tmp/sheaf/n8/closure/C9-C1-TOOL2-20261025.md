## 0. Tool II claim and verdict

Tool II (from C9-C1-NEW-TOOLS):

    Let Adm be the set of admissible integral structures S subset C_s for the
    eta^anab construction. Conjecture (Density): Adm is dense in the
    measurable subsets of C_s in a topology for which L_s and the volume-class
    map are continuous.

Verdict: REFUTED as a source-supported theorem and not usable as a C1 step.

Exact reasons:
  - There is no topology in LANA; the density statement is not well-posed as
    stated.
  - Adm is not characterized by LANA beyond "once we fix S ...", so a density
    theorem would require a new definition and a new proof, neither supplied.
  - Under the natural measure-algebra topology, the integral-structure
    condition in Adm is not dense: admissible integral structures have a
    rigid lattice/submodule character, and a model counterexample shows
    integral lattices are not dense in measurable subsets (Section 4).
  - Even if Adm were dense, density gives only that the target class is in the
    closure of L_s(Adm); it does not produce an exact S in Adm with
    [L_s(S)]=[S_q], nor eta^anab_S, nor an output D0 with logVol Q. Tool III's
    closedness/continuity/exactification hypotheses are independent and
    unproved.
Therefore Tool II does not prove C1; it is an additional unsourced
conjecture with a structural density failure.

## 1. Exact containers and source domain

C_s = VC(LS_uv), the log-shell/small volume container used as the domain of
eta^anab_S (LANA 9.2; see C9-C1-ADM-TRANSPORT, lines 2502-2517 of the LANA
report). C_l = VC(I_uv) is the large volume container, and

    Rss = { T subset C_l | T adelic and measurable } / ~,
    T ~ T' iff vol(T)=vol(T').

For a fixed S subset C_s, LANA says the anabelian construction provides an
isomorphism of pointed real lines

    eta^anab_S : Rval -> Rss,

with target pilot the class of M=1*S (the action of 1 in N), where 1*S
represents LGP*S in the large container.

The source does NOT say:
  - for which S this construction is defined beyond "once we fix S";
  - that every measurable subset of C_s is admissible;
  - that Adm is dense in any topology.
The exact admissibility predicate is itself part of the open LANA main goal.

## 2. Exact definition of Adm

We define Adm as the maximal class for which the source construction is
stated to apply. For S subset C_s:

  (A1) S is adelic measurable (in the source sense, not merely product
       measurable if the adelic/restricted-product condition is required).
  (A2) S is an integral structure compatible with the anabelian/Frobenioid
       construction: S is used as the integral structure/reference in the
       eta^anab algorithm (VC(O)-compatible/N-compatible in the sense of the
       source).
  (A3) the N-action is defined on S and 1*S is adelic measurable as a subset
       of the large container C_l; equivalently [1*S] is a class in Rss.
  (A4) [1*S] is nonzero with finite positive volume (so it is a genuine pilot
       class, not the zero class).
  (A5) eta^anab_S is defined and is a pointed R-linear isomorphism
       Rval -> Rss with target pilot [1*S].

Adm := { S subset C_s satisfying (A1)-(A5) }.

Notes:
  - (A2) is the least explicit condition in LANA; the report says S is used
    "as the integral structure" in the anabelian construction but does not
    spell out a formal predicate. Any density theorem must first make (A2)
    precise.
  - (A3)-(A5) are construction requirements, not consequences of measurability.
    C9-C1-ADM-NOGO gives the exact counterexample schema: a measurable S whose
    translate 1*S is not adelic measurable is not in Adm, even though S is a
    measurable subset of C_s. Thus Adm is a proper subclass of measurable sets
    already at the type level.
  - Adm is not known nonempty for the q-pilot case; S_q itself is target data
    in the large container, not an element of C_s (C9-C1-ADM-PROOF and
    C9-C1-DOM-S).

## 3. Topology: the density statement is not well-posed

Density depends on a topology. The source provides none on measurable subsets
of C_s. Natural candidates:

  (T1) measure-algebra topology: d_mu(S,T)=mu(S Delta T), where mu is the
       normalized Haar measure on the compact log-shell C_s; equivalent to
       L1 convergence of indicators.
  (T2) Hausdorff/Vietoris topology on closed/compact subsets of C_s.
  (T3) weak topology on L-infinity indicators.
  (T4) compact-open topology on integral structures as subspaces.

These topologies have different dense sets. For (T1), a countable family can be
dense; for (T2), the set of all compact subsets is closed/compact; for (T4),
integral lattices may be discrete. The Tool II phrase "a topology for which
L_s and the volume-class map are continuous" does not specify one. Without a
fixed topology, "Adm dense" is not a mathematical statement, only a schema.

Even if one fixes (T1), (A2)-(A5) depend on the eta^anab construction, not on
measure alone; density is not a measure-theoretic consequence.

## 4. Refutation in the natural measure topology

The following model shows the structural reason density fails for integral
structures. It is a model of the integral-structure condition (A2), not a
source-specific counterexample; it proves that "integral structures are dense
in measurable subsets" is not a general measure-theoretic fact.

Model. Let C=Z_p with normalized Haar measure mu(Z_p)=1. Let the admissible
integral structures be the compact open subgroups/lattices

    Adm_model = { p^k Z_p | k in Z }.

These are the p-adic analogue of integral structures in a log-shell. Their
volumes are mu(p^k Z_p)=p^{-k} (k in Z), a discrete subset of (0,infinity).

Choose a measurable subset A of Z_p with mu(A)=1/3. The distance in the
measure metric from A to p^k Z_p is at least

    |mu(A)-mu(p^k Z_p)| = |1/3 - p^{-k}|.

For p=2, the values p^{-k} are 1,1/2,1/4,...; the distance from 1/3 to this
set is at least 1/12 (nearest p^{-2}=1/4 gives 1/12; k->infinity gives 1/3;
k<0 gives >2/3). Therefore the open ball

    B(A,1/12) = { S measurable | mu(S Delta A) < 1/12 }

contains no p^k Z_p. The lattice class Adm_model is not dense in the
measurable subsets in the measure topology: there is a nonempty open set
disjoint from Adm_model.

The same argument applies to any admissible class whose log-volumes form a
discrete subset of R: a measurable set with volume outside the closure of that
discrete set has a positive-distance neighborhood avoiding the class. The
integral/lattice condition (A2) is precisely a rigidity condition of this
kind; it does not generate a dense family of arbitrary measurable subsets.

Caveat: the source Admissible class is not proved to equal a lattice class;
the model is not a source counterexample. It refutes the general claim that
(A2)-type integral structures are dense as a matter of measure theory, and it
identifies the exact missing statement: one would need a source theorem that
the admissible volumes/structures are dense, which LANA does not provide.

A second, source-level refutation: the statement "Adm is dense in measurable
subsets" cannot be checked without a precise (A2), and LANA explicitly says
only "once we fix S"; it does not give a density theorem. The density
conjecture therefore has no source proof; the model shows it is not automatic.

## 5. Even density would not suffice

Assume for the sake of argument that Adm is dense in the chosen topology, and
that L_s is continuous. Density gives:

    for every neighborhood of the target class [S_q], there exists S in Adm
    with [L_s(S)] in that neighborhood.

This yields only

    [S_q] in closure(L_s(Adm)).

To conclude the existence of S in Adm with [L_s(S)]=[S_q], one needs:
  - [S_q] to lie in the image L_s(Adm), not merely its closure;
  - equivalently, the image/Adm to be closed and the target to be attained.
Density alone does not give exact attainment. Tool III adds closedness,
compactness, and continuity, but those are independent unproved hypotheses
(C9-C1-TOOL3: H1-H7). Moreover, even exact class equality
[L_s(S)]=[S_q] would still need:
  - eta^anab_S defined and pointed linear (A5);
  - nonzero pilots;
  - the eta equality-to-output bridge D4, which is C1 itself.
Thus density is neither sufficient nor a reduction of C1.

## 6. Exact obstruction list

Tool II fails at the following exact points:

  O1. No topology is specified; density is not well-posed.
  O2. Adm is not characterized by LANA; (A2) is an informal "integral
      structure" condition. A density theorem would require a precise
      admissible class.
  O3. The measure-theoretic model shows integral/lattice structures are not
      dense in measurable subsets in the natural measure topology; the
      integral rigidity (A2) is not a density-generating condition.
  O4. No source theorem gives density of Adm for the actual eta^anab class.
  O5. Density gives closure, not exact attainment; closedness/image/target
      conditions (Tool III H1-H7) are unproved.
  O6. Even exact [L_s(S)]=[S_q] is not eta^anab_S defined, not pointed
      linear isomorphism, and not an output D0 with logVol Q (D3/D4).
  O7. The q-pilot target S_q is in the large container, not an element of C_s;
      density of C_s subsets does not by itself place S_q in the image.

## 7. Verdict

Tool II Density is REFUTED as a source-supported theorem and rejected as a
C1 step:
  - not well-posed without a topology;
  - Adm has no precise source characterization;
  - the natural measure topology does not make integral structures dense
    (model counterexample);
  - no LANA/IUT theorem supplies density;
  - even density would only give closure, not exact [S_q], eta^anab_S, or an
    output D0.

C1 remains open. No mixed/E3; no arbitrary topology or admissible set is
assumed; no Lean/Project edits.
