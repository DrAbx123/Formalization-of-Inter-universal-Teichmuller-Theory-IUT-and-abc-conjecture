## 2. What the companion trace theorem gives

Kondo 2025 (arXiv:2512.09231v1) Theorem E states for `k/Q_p` finite of degree
d>1 and p odd, with `Tr_{k/Q_p}: k_+ -> (Q_p)_+`:

    Ker(Tr_{k/Q_p}) = <y_1,y_3,...,y_d>_{Q_p}  (d even)
                    = <y_2,y_3,...,y_d>_{Q_p}  (d odd).       (2.1)

This identifies the trace **kernel** in the y-basis. It implies
`Tr(y_1)!=0` in the appropriate parity convention, but gives no value of
`Tr(y_1)`, no p-adic valuation, and no normalization of `y_1`.

In the C9 tame type d=e=41 odd, so the trace line is `Q_p y_1`; the kernel
is spanned by `y_2,...,y_41`. The trace functional is only determined up to
a scalar by this kernel statement; its value on `y_1` is not fixed.

## 3. The p-coefficient normalization is absent

The required normalization would be an actual source statement fixing the
primitive element `y_1` modulo units/scalars so that

    Tr(y_1)=p  (equivalently A=v_p(Tr y_1)=1),            (3.1)

or a normalization of the y-basis by a Kummer dual basis/coefficient. Kondo
2026 Lemma 1.3 only says `y_1,...,y_d` is a Q_p-basis; any nonzero Q_p-multiple
of `y_1` is equally a basis vector. Lemma 1.6/Theorem 2.6 are Q_p-linear and
scale-covariant, so they cannot fix (3.1).

Kondo 2025's coefficients c_i,e_i in the mapping-class formulas are
coefficients of other y_j in a vector v in Ker(Tr), not the p-coefficient
of y_1. They also do not normalize Tr(y_1).

The Y1/Kummer source obstruction (task-217/E35 etc.) remains: Kummer theory
supplies finite-order torsion data, while y_1=F(x_1) is a pro-p log; it does
not produce a dual-basis normalization of Tr(y_1).

## 4. Consequence for kappa and B

With

    kappa := e v_p(Tr y_1) - v_pi(y_1),   A:=v_p(Tr y_1),   B:=v_pi(y_1),

the conditional branch theorem is

    kappa<=0:       H_A-full, B=O_K;
    1<=kappa<=40:   B=I;
    kappa>=41:      branch not well-posed inside I.              (4.1)

Kondo 2026 gives neither A nor B. What is available:

- `Tr(y_1)!=0` and `Tr(y_1)` in `pZ_p` from the kernel/integrality
  arguments, so `A>=1`;
- `y_1` in `J=m`, so `B>=1`; hence the structural lower bound `kappa>=0`
  but no upper bound `kappa<=40`.

If one added the missing normalization `A=1`, then
`kappa=41-B`, and `B>=1` gives `kappa<=40`; for `B<=40` (equivalently
`y_1` not in `pO_K`) one gets `1<=kappa<=40` and B=I. But `A=1` is exactly
the missing p-coefficient normalization; it is not in Kondo 2026.

Equivalently, the conditional implication

    (A=1 and y_1 not in pO_K)  =>  B=I                       (4.2)

is valid, but its hypothesis is unproved. Without it, B=I is not proved;
B=O_K is not proved either. The branch remains undetermined.

## 5. Exact obstruction

Theorem 5.1. Kondo 2026 does not imply `B=I` (nor `B=O_K`) for the C9 tame
type. Its exact missing input is the actual `y_1`/`Tr(y_1)` p-coefficient
normalization; equivalently `A=1` (and `y_1` not in `pO_K`) or an equivalent
Kummer dual-basis normalization.

Proof. Kondo 2026's statements are (i) a Q_p-basis `y_1,...,y_d`, (ii)
Q_p-linearity/closedness of Phi, (iii) Hodge-Tate coefficient criterion
`alpha_+=sum i_sigma sigma`, and (iv) Sp containment. None fixes the scalar
of `y_1` or evaluates `Tr(y_1)`. Kondo 2025 Theorem E gives only the trace
kernel, not the value. The branch theorem (4.1) needs `kappa`, which depends
on the unnormalized `Tr(y_1)` and `v_pi(y_1)`. A structural model with
`kappa=81` (Task-328) satisfies the recorded kernel/line/J-integrality data
but violates `kappa<=40`, so those data cannot imply B=I. QED.
