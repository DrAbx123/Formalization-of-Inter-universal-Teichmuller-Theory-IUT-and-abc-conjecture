Sep 11, 2026 · Claim Steward · after staleness check Reassessed: Supported → Verified verdict confidence 0.75 · credence 0.88 Corollary 3.12 of the third Inter-universal Teichmüller Theory paper is the step at which Mochizuki s argument produces the inequality that implies the abc conjecture, and its proof, the passage from Theorem 3.11 to the corollary, is where every serious examination of the papers has located a missing step. Three independent examinations, by different methods and with different sympathies, have now reached that finding. In 2018 Peter Scholze and Jakob Stix reported, after a week of discussion with Mochizuki in Kyoto, that under their simplifying identifications the argument yields only a trivial inequality ↗︎ ; Scholze repeated after formal publication that the argument given is not a proof. Kirti Joshi, a critic of the Scholze–Stix reasoning who has built a rival framework sympathetic to Mochizuki s ideas, maintains that the papers do not establish the corollary as written ↗︎ . In July 2026 the LANA project, a Lean formalization effort led by Fumiharu Kato that worked inside Mochizuki s own framework and put its questions to Mochizuki and his collaborator Yuichiro Hoshi directly over two years, reported that the passage depends on an unproven compatibility between the q-pilot and multiradial constructions ↗︎ and that it had not been able to reconstruct a proof of it; Kato stated that the argument as written is unformalizable. Hoshi had already acknowledged, at the project s March 2026 launch, that many LANA members saw an insurmountable wall at exactly this step and that he had not succeeded in dispelling it. In fourteen years no expert outside Mochizuki s circle has affirmed that the proof is complete, and the mathematical community broadly does not accept it ↗︎ . The evidence against is of two kinds. The papers were refereed and published in Publications of RIMS ↗︎ in 2021, which is a fact but a weak one here: the journal is edited at Mochizuki s home institute, the refereeing is unpublished, and post-publication expert review maintained the objection. Mochizuki s rebuttal holds that Scholze and Stix reached their conclusion only by identifying objects the theory keeps distinct ↗︎ , and the LANA report gives that rebuttal partial support on one point: it finds that the monodromy inconsistency Scholze and Stix exhibit does not arise in a formulation that keeps the theory s distinctions ↗︎ . But this weakens one route to the gap without closing it, since LANA s own analysis, which preserves those distinctions, still finds the final compatibility unproven. Joshi in turn disputes LANA s diagnosis while reaffirming that the papers do not supply what the proof needs. The pattern across all the dissent is the same: the critics of each account of the gap attack the account, not the conclusion that the published proof falls short. Two things remain open and should be read alongside the verdict. Fundamental is taken here to mean a gap in the essential mechanism, the step on which the entire inequality hinges, rather than a repairable slip elsewhere; that is where all three examinations place it. Whether the gap can eventually be filled is a different question: LANA describes the missing compatibility as not manifestly false and has reserved final judgment because Mochizuki s explanations continue to evolve. A proof of that compatibility, accepted by the formalization team or by independent experts, would show the published proof to have been incomplete rather than unsalvageable, and would call for this claim to be reread in that light; an independent endorsement of Mochizuki s position that no step is missing would reopen it entirely. Aug 24, 2026 · Claim Steward · after initial assessment Assessed Supported verdict confidence 0.75 · credence 0.85 Corollary 3.12 of the third IUT paper is the step where Mochizuki s abstract machinery is meant to produce the concrete inequality behind the abc conjecture, and the charge that its proof contains a fundamental gap is the central issue in the long-running dispute over whether that conjecture has been proved. The charge originates with Peter Scholze and Jakob Stix, who studied the papers, spent a week discussing them with Mochizuki in Kyoto in 2018, and reported that under their simplifying identifications the argument yields only a trivial inequality ↗︎ , a problem they judged too severe for small modifications to repair. Scholze maintained the position after the papers formal publication, writing in his zbMATH review that the argument given for the corollary is not a proof. The years since have strengthened rather than weakened the charge s standing. The mathematical community broadly does not accept the IUT proof ↗︎ , and no independent expert has verified the proof of Corollary 3.12 in the thirteen years the papers have circulated. Notably, Kirti Joshi, whose own program is sympathetic to Mochizuki s central intuitions and critical of Scholze and Stix s reasoning, likewise concludes that the papers do not establish Corollary 3.12 as written ↗︎ , locating the gap in the papers failure to prove that the distinct arithmetic structures the argument averages over actually exist. Two considerations cut the other way. Mochizuki maintains that the objection rests on wrongly identifying objects his theory treats as distinct ↗︎ , a rebuttal that remains contested but has persuaded no independent arithmetic geometer. And the papers were peer-reviewed and published in Publications of RIMS ↗︎ in 2021, though the journal is housed at Mochizuki s own institute and the refereeing has not persuaded outside experts. A narrower dissent comes from Taylor Dupuy, who argues that the simplified statement Scholze and Stix refute is not what Corollary 3.12 actually asserts ↗︎ , though Dupuy s own work treats the corollary as unproven rather than proven. The evidence therefore favors the claim without settling it beyond dispute: every independent expert who has taken a public position holds that the proof as written is at least incomplete, while the deny side consists of Mochizuki, close colleagues, and an opaque refereeing process. The question would be resolved by an independent verification or formalization of the proof, by broad acceptance of a completion such as Joshi claims, or by a demonstration, accepted outside Kyoto, that maintaining the distinctions Mochizuki insists on blocks the Scholze–Stix reduction.
