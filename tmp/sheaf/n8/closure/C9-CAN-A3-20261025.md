## 2. L1: canonical full-poly-isomorphism invariance

Lemma L1 (canonical). If two F^⊩×μ-prime-strips S,S' are linked by a full poly-isomorphism, then their canonical pilot log-volumes are equal:
    L_can(S)=L_can(S').
In particular, the canonical pilot log-volume of the q-pilot representation in the output data linked to the fixed input S_q equals Q_can.

Proof/source support.
- Packet-normalization: multiplication by e adds 1 (line 6769); unit/scalar isomorphisms of the log-shells preserve this.
- Procession-normalization: average over capsules (lines 6776-6778); a full poly-isomorphism permutes the capsule factors/labels in F_l^x, and the average is permutation-invariant.
- Global log-volume: additive over places; invariant under permutations of A and multiplication by units (lines 6836-6843); compatible with the natural poly-isomorphisms (lines 6779-6815).
- The full poly-isomorphism is a natural poly-isomorphism of F^⊩×μ-prime-strips; the canonical log-volume is defined on these prime-strips, so the compatibility gives equality.

Exact source gap in L1: Cor3.12 does not display the sentence "canonical log-volume is invariant under full poly-isomorphism"; it follows from Prop3.9(i),(ii) and the definition of the full poly-isomorphism. A formal proof must identify the full poly-isomorphisms with the natural poly-isomorphisms of Prop3.9(ii). This is a source-expansion gap, not a normalized surrogate.

## 4. L3: canonical hull/log-volume finiteness and bound

Lemma L3 (canonical). Under the proof assumptions of Theorem B:
(a) h_can is finite (hence in R), so R_{<= h_can} is defined;
(b) every possible canonical output log-volume lambda in R_poss satisfies lambda <= h_can; i.e. R_poss subset R_{<= h_can}.

Proof/source support.
(a) The compactness of 1,circU_j,vQ for j in |F_l| and vQ in VQ, together with the definition of the canonical log-volume, implies -|log Theta| finite; this is exactly lines 10234-10242 (after WLOG -|log Theta|<0). The hull is a hull of compact regions; the procession average over j is finite.
(b) The holomorphic hull 1,circU contains the union of possible images 1,circU. Canonical log-volume is monotone under inclusion (Prop3.9/Remark3.9.5; the hull is the least candidate hull of Remark3.9.5). Therefore the canonical log-volume of every possible output image is at most the canonical log-volume h_can of the hull; R_poss subset R_{<= h_can}.

Exact source gap in L3: Cor3.12 cites Propositions 3.9/Remark3.9.5 for finiteness and the hull operation but does not display the inclusion/monotonicity proof; it is source-expansion, not a normalized surrogate. ## 6. Exact approximation gap G1

The source's xi-f parenthetical is the only unresolved wording:

If "perhaps only up to approximation" is read literally, then L2 does not give exact membership; instead one has only

for some unspecified canonical error epsilon. The source provides:
- no bound on |epsilon|;
- no sign of epsilon;
- no comparison epsilon vs h_can - Q_can.
Therefore an exact inequality Q_can <= h_can cannot be derived from the approximation reading without an additional source theorem. Choosing a bound or sign for epsilon would be arbitrary and is not allowed.

Consequently canonical A3 has exactly two possibilities:

(ii) exact gap G1, if the approximation wording is taken literally; then no explicit epsilon is available and P6_can is not proved from A3.
No normalized surrogate and no factor d=41 can bridge G1: dividing Q_can by d or replacing h_can by h_C changes the target, not the gap.
