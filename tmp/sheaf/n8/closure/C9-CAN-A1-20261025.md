## 1. Canonical A1 statement

**Definition (canonical A1).** In the situation of Theorem3.11, the canonical q-pilot/BPS input is

    A1 := ( [F^value x mu] , Q_pilot , Q_can , Z_0 ),             (1.1)

where:

1. `[F^value x mu]` is the full poly-isomorphism class of F^⊩▶×mu-prime-strips participating in the LGP-link; no representative is chosen.

## 2. Canonicality theorem

**Theorem (A1 canonical).** Each entry of (1.1) is well-defined independently of the admissible generator/torsion/poly-isomorphism choices.

(a) `Q_pilot` is independent, up to canonical isomorphism, of the collection of generators up to torsion: this is exactly Definition3.8(i)'s `up to torsion` clause and the realified Frobenioid construction. A scalar `q_v` is not canonical, but `Q_pilot` is.
(b) `Q_can=-|log q|` is independent of the generator/torsion choice because it is computed from the realified object `Q_pilot`, and independent of the full-poly representative because it is a full-poly-invariant normalized log-volume.
(c) `[F^value x mu]` is by definition a full poly-isomorphism class; the LGP-link is the full poly-isomorphism of Definition3.8(ii), not a chosen map.
(d) `Z_0` is the source-defined 0-column; its construction uses the unit portion, bi-coricity and log-Kummer correspondences, not an arbitrary section.

Proof. (a) is Definition3.8(i): the q-pilot is determined by any collection of generators up to torsion, i.e. the object is independent of the collection. (b) follows since `Q_can` is a function of `Q_pilot` and its normalized log-volume is invariant under full poly-isomorphism. (c) is Definition3.8(ii). (d) is Cor.3.12 Steps (iii)-(v). QED.
